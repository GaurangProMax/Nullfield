01 — The Concept
What is a Chrono-Jump?

The word 'Chrono' means something related to time. 'Jump' is an action that makes you travel in space — and by space, I mean the physical or materialistic world around us.

In my view, a Jump either means to travel and ascend along the X or Y axis — or to skip a certain series of time by going backwards or forwards through it. Together, a Chrono-Jump is the act of travelling through time itself, using the warping of spacetime as the mechanism.

This theory heavily involves black holes. So before we can understand the jump, we need to understand the wonderful creations of nature that make it possible.

"A Chrono-Jump is not science fiction — it is a question of engineering. The physics already exists. The distance is the only problem."

02 — The Engine
The Formation of Black Holes

Imagine shrinking a star — let's say the size of our Sun. You keep shrinking and shrinking it until it becomes small enough to have a radius of just 3 kilometres. This is called the Schwarzschild radius. At this point something extraordinary happens.

The density becomes so extreme that this is like taking a star away from its place without removing the distortion it caused in spacetime. The gravitational imprint remains, but the star is gone — leaving only a hole in the fabric of the universe.

Black holes therefore have so much gravity that their escape velocity — the speed required to escape the atmosphere of a celestial body — exceeds the speed of light. Hence, the light that goes into a black hole never comes out. It simply revolves around the black hole in what is called the accretion disk.

01
Escape Velocity
The speed an object needs to break free from a gravitational field without further propulsion. For a black hole, this exceeds the speed of light — making escape physically impossible for anything, including light.
02
Accretion Disk
The glowing ring of matter orbiting a black hole at extreme velocities. Light and matter that come close but don't cross the event horizon get trapped here, spiralling inward over time.
03
Event Horizon
The point of no return around a black hole. Once any object — matter, light, information — crosses this boundary, it cannot escape. It is not a physical surface but a mathematical boundary in spacetime.
04
Singularity
The point at the very end of a wormhole — the last point of time, where the laws of physics as we understand them completely break down. Density becomes infinite, and spacetime curvature becomes infinite.
03 — The Fabric
Spacetime and Wormholes

Space and time are not separate things — they are very similar, and together they form a material called Spacetime. This is also known as the fabric of our universe.

This godly material corresponds to the mass and energy of an object, causing distortions. Think of it like placing a heavy ball on a stretched rubber sheet — the sheet warps around it. The heavier the object, the deeper the distortion. These distortions in spacetime are what we experience as gravity.

When the distortions are extreme enough — such as near a black hole — these distortions make wormholes. Since both space AND time are being warped simultaneously, the very end of these wormholes appears to be the last point of time, known as singularity — the end of time itself.

However, these wormholes appear to be connected. The universe stores information, and hence the objects sucked inside a black hole must get out from the other end. As information breaks down on its way to the singularity — the object stretches long, then molecules form a line, then atoms separate — they race to the other end.

04 — The Jump
How a Chrono-Jump Works
01
Entry into the Black Hole
An object enters a black hole and crosses the event horizon. At this point, spacetime curvature begins breaking down the object's information — stretching it long, then into a molecular line, then separating atoms. This process is called spaghettification.
02
Travel Through the Wormhole
The broken-down information races through the interconnected wormhole toward the other end of the black hole. The singularity — the end of time — connects to another point in spacetime through this tunnel.
03
Reverse-Chronological Restoration
When the contents reach the other part of the interconnected wormhole, they seem to go outwards — possibly because of the positive and negative polarity of the field. As they emerge, they experience every event they underwent while going in, but reverse-chronologically. The equation of distortion repeats and becomes the complete opposite — restoring the object.
04
Emergence at a Different Point in Time
Because spacetime itself was warped during the transit, the object doesn't just emerge at a different location in space — it emerges at a different point in time. The direction of the jump (forward or backward) is determined by the geometry of the specific wormhole traversed.
